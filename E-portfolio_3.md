Artefact #1: Robodebt scheme
Link: https://robodebt.royalcommission.gov.au/system/files/2023-09/rrc-accessible-full-report.PDF

Summary:
The Royal Comission into the Robodebt scheme was established in August, 2022 in order to investigate devastating consequences of this scheme on the economy and the public. The scheme relied on a process known as "income averaging" to assess income and entitlement to benefit. Moreover, the process assumed that everyone received a steady, consistent income each week, which is entirely wrong for casual or seasonal workers (detached from reality) (Commonwealth of Australia 2023). To make it worse, public servants were mostly ignored or discouraged when they raised concerns about it, which the system failed to account for human oversight and objective judegement. 

Personal reflection:
This artefact was chosen because it is directly related to the main theme. First, RPA (or Robotic process automation) is used to automate repetitive office tasks and rule-based processes conducted by humans (Chugh, Macht & Hossain 2022); However, robots are designed to perform centain tasks, it was not capable of handling ones which require logical reasoning. While benefit of RPA is increased speed and productivity, RPA was able to scale debt notice issuance from 20,000 annually to 20,000 per week, highlighting a hurdle of automated decision-making where lack of transparency and proper scrutiny caused significant harm to the economy, society or the public. To tackle this, business rules and algorithms need to be documented, transparent, and available for independent expert review and establish body to monitor and audit automated decision-making (Commonwealth of Australia 2023).
